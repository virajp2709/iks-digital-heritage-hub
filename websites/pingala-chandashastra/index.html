छन्दःशास्त्रPingala’s Science of Meter

The Chandaḥśāstra of Pingala (c. 3rd–2nd century BCE) classifies Sanskrit meter with just two kinds of syllable, short and long, and so lays down ideas we now call binary numbers, combinatorics and Pascal’s triangle.

“छन्दः पादौ तु वेदस्य”Pāṇinīya Śikṣā: “Chandas is the feet of the Veda”

1Introduction to Chhanda Shastra

Chhanda means meter, and Chhandaśāstra is the science of poetic meter, or prosody. This site presents its mathematical dimension, following the paper “Chhanda Shastra of Pingla – A Mathematical Review” (Mrs Shefali Joshi & Anant Vyawahare). Tap a card to flip it.

इतिहास

2History & Contribution

Contributions of Pingalacharya

Attributed to him is the first text, Chhandaśāstra, revealing the binary code, the Fibonacci series, the Matra Meru (Pascal’s triangle) and the rules for transforming a verse into any meter.

Reader’s note. Dates and claims on this page (Pingala ~300 BC; Panini as elder sibling, ~750 BC; Rigveda as old as 8000 BC) are as stated in the paper. Many historians date Pingala to about the 3rd–2nd century BCE, the Rigveda to about 1500–1200 BCE, and treat Panini’s family link as tradition. The Fibonacci-type recurrence is stated clearly in the commentary tradition (Virahāṅka, ~7th c. CE) on Pingala’s rule.

4 · Laghu (0) & Guru (1)
0
Laghu (short) = 1 mātrā
short vowels a, i, u
G
1
Guru (long) = 2 mātrās
long vowels aa, ii, uu, ai, au
  • A syllable is laghu if it has a short vowel (a, i, u).
  • The combined vowels aa, ii, uu, ai, au are long, so the syllable is guru.
  • Even a short syllable becomes guru if it has a final consonant with an anusvāra or a visarga.
  • The last syllable of a line may optionally be taken as guru.
  • Textbook addition: e and o are also long, and a short vowel before a conjunct consonant is guru (the scanner below uses these rules).
5 · Types of Sanskrit Meter

🔹 Akṣara-chhanda

syllabic meter: varṇa / gaṇa vṛtta

The number of syllables is specified. The Vedic akṣara- chhanda, in short “chhanda”, is named by its syllable count. Also called vṛtta chhanda: a verse of 4 lines, each with a specified sequence of long and short syllables.

🔹 Mātrā chhanda

moric meter: mātrā vṛtta

A short syllable gets 1 mātrā, a long syllable 2 mātrās. The total number of mātrās is specified (in the paper’s words, in gaṇa-chhanda meters the number of mātrās in each line is specified).

Pingala’s algorithms deal only with vṛtta chhanda, which is of three types:
TypeMeaning
Sama (equal)all four lines (pādas) follow the same pattern
Ardhasama (half equal)1st = 3rd line and 2nd = 4th line
Viṣama (unequal)the lines differ

Vedic examples: Gāyatrī 3 × 8 = 24 syllables · Anuṣṭubh 4 × 8 = 32 · Triṣṭubh 4 × 11 = 44 · Jagatī 4 × 12 = 48.

7 · Fibonacci Sequence & its Formula
No. of mātrās123456789…
Variations1235813213455…
Tn = Tn−1 + Tn−2,   n > 2

Tn is the n-th term (T1 = 1, T2 = 2). This is the Fibonacci sequence of L. Fibonacci (1170–1250, Italy). Closed form (extra): Tn = (φn+1 − ψn+1)/√5, with φ = (1+√5)/ 2, ψ = (1−√5)/2.

Pingala states it in the sutra “Aṅkatyopāntya yoga pare pare mātrāṇām” (as quoted in the paper):
the sum of the last two numbers is the count for the next mātrā vṛtta.

8 · Number Partitions (with examples)

The numbers of the Fibonacci sequence are the number of partitions of that number, which, the paper notes, reflects Ramanujan’s partition theory. Example: 4 has 5 partitions, which are the variations of mātrā 4:

Careful: these are ordered ways of writing n as 1s and 2s (1 = laghu, 2 = guru), which is why they give Fibonacci numbers. Ordinary integer partitions differ from n = 5 on: p(5) = 7, but T5 = 8.

9Ordinary Binary Representation

Any natural number can be written in base 2 as a string of 0s and 1s using the remainders in successive division by 2. Example: (14)10 = (1110)2 = 0·2⁰ + 1·2¹ + 1·2² + 1·2³ = 14. It uses 4 digits.

10 · Pingala Moric Binary Code

Pingala used the quotients in the division instead. Even number: write laghu (0) and halve. Odd number: write guru (1), add 1 to make it even, then halve. At 1, write guru (1) and stop. For 14 this gives 01001 (5 digits).

Try any number:

The paper’s abstract prints 14 as (10110), the same code with 0 and 1 swapped; its worked method (and this page) gives 01001. Decoding: row number = 1 + place values (1, 2, 4, 8…) of the laghu positions.

मेरुप्रस्तार

11Meru-Prastāra (Pascal’s Triangle)

The Meru-prastāra (triangular extension) of 7 varṇas, in reference to the number of mātrās. Pingala called it varṇa meru (also mātrā meru). It is also known as Pascal’s triangle (Blaise Pascal, 1623–1662, France). Each row sums to 2n.

12Permutations (Aṅkapāśa)

Pingala called permutations aṅkapāśa. He posed the question: in how many ways can the digits of the number 123 be put without repetition?

1 × 2 × 3 = 6 = 3!

The 4-digit number 7289 can be arranged in 1 × 2 × 3 × 4 = 24 = 4! ways.
